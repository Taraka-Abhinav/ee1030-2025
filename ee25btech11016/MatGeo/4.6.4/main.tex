\textbf{Question}:\\
Find the equation of a line passing through the point $(2, 3, 2)$ and parallel to the line $\vec{r} = (-2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}) + \lambda(2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k})$. Also, find the distance between these two lines.

\solution \\

\textbf{1. Equation of the Line}\\
The equation is $\vec{r} = \vec{a_2} + \mu\vec{b}$.
\begin{align}
    \vec{r} = \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2} + \mu\myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}
\end{align}

\textbf{2. Distance Between Lines}\\
The distance is given by the formula:
\begin{align}
    d = \frac{\|\vec{b} \times (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})\|}{\|\vec{b}\|}
\end{align}
First, we find the required vectors algebraically. Let $\Delta\vec{a} = \vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1}$. Let the cross product be $\vec{v} = \vec{b} \times \Delta\vec{a}$. The formula is $d = \frac{\|\vec{v}\|}{\|\vec{b}\|}$.\\

Now, we substitute the numerical values:
\begin{align}
    \vec{a_1} = \myvec{-2 \\ 3 \\ 0}, \quad \vec{a_2} = \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2}, \quad \vec{b} = \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
    \Delta\vec{a} = \myvec{4 \\ 0 \\ 2}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
    \vec{v} = \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6} \times \myvec{4 \\ 0 \\ 2} = \myvec{-6 \\ 20 \\ 12}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
    \|\vec{v}\| &= \sqrt{(-6)^2 + 20^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{580} = 2\sqrt{145} \\
    \|\vec{b}\| &= \sqrt{2^2 + (-3)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{49} = 7
\end{align}
The distance is:
\begin{align}
    d = \frac{2\sqrt{145}}{7}
\end{align}
