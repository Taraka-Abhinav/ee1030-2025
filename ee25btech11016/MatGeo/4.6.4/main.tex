\textbf{Question}:\\
Find the equation of a line passing through the point $\brak{2, 3, 2}$ and parallel to the line $\vec{r} = \brak{-2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}} + \lambda\brak{2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}}$. Also, find the distance between these two lines. \hfill $\brak{12, 2018}$
\\

\solution \\

\textbf{Part 1: Equation of the Parallel Line}\\
This remains the same regardless of the method for finding the distance.
\begin{align}
\vec{a}_2 = \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2}, \quad \vec{b} = \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\implies \text{Line Equation is } \boxed{\vec{r} = \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2} + \mu \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}}
\end{align}
\textbf{Part 2: Distance using Least Squares Method}\\
The distance is the magnitude of the error vector when projecting the connecting vector $\vec{w} = \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1$ onto the direction vector $\vec{b}$.
The vectors are:
\begin{align}
\vec{a}_1 = \myvec{-2 \\ 3 \\ 0}, \quad \vec{w} = \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1 = \myvec{4 \\ 0 \\ 2}
\end{align}
The least squares solution for the projection scalar $t$ is found from the normal equation:
\begin{align}
(\vec{b}^T\vec{b})t = \vec{b}^T\vec{w}
\end{align}
Calculating the dot products:
\begin{align}
\vec{b}^T\vec{b} &= \norm{\vec{b}}^2 = (2)^2 + (-3)^2 + (6)^2 = 49 \\
\vec{b}^T\vec{w} &= (2)(4) + (-3)(0) + (6)(2) = 20
\end{align}
Solving for $t$:
\begin{align}
49t = 20 \implies t = \frac{20}{49}
\end{align}
The shortest distance vector is the error vector, $\vec{d} = \vec{w} - t\vec{b}$.
\begin{align}
\vec{d} &= \myvec{4 \\ 0 \\ 2} - \frac{20}{49}\myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6} = \myvec{4 - 40/49 \\ 60/49 \\ 2 - 120/49} = \frac{1}{49}\myvec{156 \\ 60 \\ -22}
\end{align}
The distance is the magnitude (norm) of this error vector:
\begin{align}
d = \norm{\vec{d}} = \frac{1}{49}\sqrt{156^2 + 60^2 + (-22)^2} = \frac{\sqrt{28420}}{49} = \frac{14\sqrt{145}}{49}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\implies d = \boxed{\frac{2\sqrt{145}}{7}}
\end{align}
