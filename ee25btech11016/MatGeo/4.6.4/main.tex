\textbf{Question}:\\
Find the equation of a line passing through the point $(2, 3, 2)$ and parallel to the line $\vec{r} = (-2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}) + \lambda(2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k})$. Also, find the distance between these two lines.
\\

\solution \\
The problem is solved in two parts. All vectors are represented using the \verb|\myvec| command.

\subsection*{Part 1: Equation of the Required Line}
The vector equation of a line is $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + \mu\vec{b}$, where $\vec{a}$ is the position vector of a point on the line and $\vec{b}$ is the direction vector of the line.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Position Vector ($\vec{a}$):} The line passes through the point $(2, 3, 2)$. We can write its position vector as:
    \begin{align}
    \mat{a} = \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2}
    \end{align}
    
    \item \textbf{Direction Vector ($\vec{b}$):} The required line is parallel to the given line, so they share the same direction vector. From the given equation, the direction vector is:
    \begin{align}
    \mat{b} = \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}
    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
Combining these gives the equation of the required line:
\begin{align}
\vec{r} = \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2} + \mu \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}
\end{align}
\subsection*{Part 2: Distance Between the Lines}
The distance is found by identifying points $\mat{P} = \mat{a} + p_1\mat{d}$ and $\mat{Q} = \mat{b} + p_2\mat{d}$ on each line such that the vector $\mat{Q}-\mat{P}$ is orthogonal to the direction $\mat{d}$.

\subsubsection*{1. Define Vectors}
The problem defines the two parallel lines. We can represent them with the following vectors:
\begin{align}
\mat{a} &= \myvec{-2 \\ 3 \\ 0} & & \text{(Point on Line 1)} \\
\mat{b} &= \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2} & & \text{(Point on Line 2)} \\
\mat{d} &= \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6} & & \text{(Common Direction)}
\end{align}

\subsubsection*{2. Solve for Parameters}
The orthogonality condition is $\mat{d}^\top(\mat{Q}-\mat{P}) = 0$. Expanding this gives the equation for the parameters:
\begin{align}
(\mat{d}^\top\mat{d})(p_2-p_1) + \mat{d}^\top(\mat{b}-\mat{a}) = 0
\end{align}
We calculate the scalar products:
\begin{align}
\mat{d}^\top\mat{d} &= \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}^\top \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6} = 4 + 9 + 36 = 49 \\
\mat{d}^\top(\mat{b}-\mat{a}) &= \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}^\top \left( \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2} - \myvec{-2 \\ 3 \\ 0} \right) \nonumber \\
&= \myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6}^\top \myvec{4 \\ 0 \\ 2} = 8 + 0 + 12 = 20
\end{align}
Substituting these values back:
\begin{align}
49(p_2 - p_1) + 20 &= 0 \\
p_2 - p_1 &= -\frac{20}{49} \\
\implies p_1 - p_2 &= \frac{20}{49}
\end{align}
We need one pair of values that satisfy this. We can simply take $\mathbf{p_2 = 0}$, which gives $\mathbf{p_1 = 20/49}$.

\subsubsection*{3. Calculate the Distance}
Using these parameter values, we find the specific points $\mat{P}$ and $\mat{Q}$.
\begin{align}
\mat{P} &= \mat{a} + p_1\mat{d} = \myvec{-2 \\ 3 \\ 0} + \frac{20}{49}\myvec{2 \\ -3 \\ 6} = \myvec{-58/49 \\ 87/49 \\ 120/49} \\
\mat{Q} &= \mat{b} + p_2\mat{d} = \myvec{2 \\ 3 \\ 2}
\end{align}
The vector connecting these points is:
\begin{align}
\mat{P}-\mat{Q} = \myvec{-58/49 - 98/49 \\ 87/49 - 147/49 \\ 120/49 - 98/49} = \frac{1}{49}\myvec{-156 \\ -60 \\ 22}
\end{align}
The distance is the norm of this vector, $||\mat{P}-\mat{Q}||$.
\begin{align}
d^2 &= \left\|\frac{1}{49}\myvec{-156 \\ -60 \\ 22}\right\|^2 \nonumber \\
&= \left(\frac{1}{49}\right)^2 \left( (-156)^2 + (-60)^2 + (22)^2 \right) \nonumber \\
&= \frac{1}{2401} (24336 + 3600 + 484) = \frac{28420}{2401} = \frac{580}{49} \\
d &= \sqrt{\frac{580}{49}} = \frac{\sqrt{4 \times 145}}{7}
\end{align}
The final distance is:
\begin{align}
\implies d = \boxed{\frac{2\sqrt{145}}{7}}
\end{align}
